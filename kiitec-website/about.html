Training Tanzania's own technicians since 2004.

Don Bosco Kilimanjaro International Institute for Telecommunications, Electronics and Computers. KIITEC for short.

Our story

Built to fix a real problem.

KIITEC began with a simple observation. A group of French engineers staying at an international hotel in Arusha saw that broken lifts and fax machines had to wait for technicians from abroad before they could be repaired.

In 2004, together with the French philanthropist Francis Brochon, they founded an institute to train skilled technicians in Tanzania. It grew with international partners, including FTE (Switzerland) and ADEI (France).

Today the Salesians of Don Bosco run the institute. It brings telecommunications, electronics and computing together with electrical engineering and new fields like AI, data science and robotics.

Milestones

How KIITEC has grown

  1. 2004

    KIITEC is founded

    French engineers and philanthropist Francis Brochon open the institute in Arusha to train local technicians.

  2. Registered

    NACTVET registration

    KIITEC is registered as a technical institute (REG/EOS/027). Graduates earn a three-year diploma at NTA Level 6.

  3. 2024

    New student hostel

    A hostel with space for 300 students is completed, funded by a local benefactor and Salesian Missions.

  4. 2025

    Joins the IUS network

    KIITEC joins the Salesian Institutions of Higher Education network, with about 250 students and four diploma programmes.

  5. 2026

    Emerging technology diplomas

    New diplomas in AI & Machine Learning, Data Science and Robotics & Drones open, part of a plan to serve more than 3,000 students.

Our approach

From understanding a concept to making it work.

Technical skill grows through practice, reflection and working with others. Every programme follows the same path.

  1. Understand the principles

    Build a solid grounding in your field, linking classroom theory to the systems you will use in the lab.

  2. Learn at the workbench

    Work on circuits, computers, control systems and energy technology. Develop care, accuracy and a methodical approach.

  3. Connect learning to work

    Student projects and industrial attachment build teamwork, communication and real workplace habits.

Mission

Educating the whole person.

KIITEC follows the Salesian tradition of St John Bosco, who believed young people thrive through good teaching, trust and responsibility. The institute serves young people from all backgrounds, including those who could not otherwise afford technical training.

Alongside engineering, students build entrepreneurship skills, discipline and the confidence to take on working life.

The institute is led by its director, Fr. Sebastian Chirayath.
